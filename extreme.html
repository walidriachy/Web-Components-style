Ten motion studies · zero libraries

Every scene on this page is hand-written canvas and raw maths. No GSAP, no Three.js, no chart library, nothing from a CDN except two typefaces. Move your cursor through the letters.

02 — Displacement

Liquid chrome

An SVG feTurbulence noise field driving feDisplacementMap over gradient-clipped text. The noise frequency animates, so the metal actually flows rather than just sliding a gradient.

MOLTEN

03 — Implicit surfaces

Metaballs

Six charges summed per pixel on a downsampled field, thresholded and marched into a contour. They genuinely merge and separate — this is a scalar field, not overlapping circles.

Move the cursor

04 — Force field

Magnetic grid

Two thousand nodes, each with a spring back to its origin and an inverse-square repulsion from the cursor. Displacement drives colour and scale, so the field reads as depth.

Push through it

05 — Perspective

Holo card

Real 3D rotation on a perspective ancestor, with a specular highlight tracking the pointer and a conic foil layer in color-dodge that rotates with tilt. Releases with a spring.

06 — Vector field

Flow

Three thousand particles advected through a value-noise curl field. Trails come from compositing a translucent black rectangle each frame rather than clearing — cheap, and it looks like ink.

Cursor bends the field

07 — Channel separation

Datamosh

Text rendered once, then torn into horizontal slices with per-slice offsets and independent red/cyan channel displacement composited in lighter. Bursts on a Poisson-ish schedule.

08 — Mechanism

Split flap

Each character steps through the alphabet with a real half-card fold — two stacked faces rotating about the horizontal centre, staggered per column.

Cycles every few seconds

09 — Scroll velocity

Inertial marquee

Base drift plus scroll velocity, with skew derived from that velocity and eased back to zero. Scroll hard and the type leans; stop and it settles.

11 — WebGL · GLSL

Raymarched SDF

A real fragment shader. Every pixel marches a ray through a signed-distance field of a twisted torus and a sphere, smooth-unioned together, then shades it with an analytic normal from the gradient. This is the same technique demoscene shaders use.

Cursor steers the camera

12 — Navier–Stokes

Fluid dye

A semi-Lagrangian advection solver. The cursor injects velocity and dye into a grid; each step traces backwards along the velocity field and samples with bilinear interpolation, so the dye genuinely swirls.

Drag to inject dye

13 — Gray–Scott

Reaction diffusion

Two chemicals, one feeding and one killing, each diffusing at a different rate across a Laplacian kernel. Nobody draws these patterns — they emerge from the equations. Several solver steps run per frame.

Cursor seeds new reagent

14 — Verlet integration

Cloth

A lattice of point masses joined by distance constraints, relaxed over several iterations per frame. Gravity pulls, the cursor drags, and the constraint solver keeps the weave together.

Drag the fabric

15 — Escape-time

Infinite zoom

The Mandelbrot set with smooth continuous colouring — the fractional escape iteration rather than the integer, so there is no banding. It zooms forever toward a seahorse valley and resets.

Zooming continuously

16 — Web Audio · FFT

Spectrum

Three detuned oscillators through a moving filter, analysed with a real FFT. The bars are actual frequency-bin magnitudes, not a fake sine wave.

17 — Parametric 3D

Torus knot

A (3,7) torus knot generated parametrically, rotated by hand-written matrices and projected to 2D with perspective divide. Depth-sorted and shaded by z. No 3D library.

Cursor rotates it

18 — Wave equation

Ripple tank

A discrete height field integrating the 2D wave equation with damping. Disturbances propagate, reflect off the walls and interfere with each other exactly as real waves do.

Move to make waves
